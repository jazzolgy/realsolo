This does **not** prove that every extra onset is a drum hit. It does show a strong
whole-ensemble positional arc in this source: relatively restrained openings,
more active/bright middle spans, then an average reduction near the end.

For drummer modeling this supports a hypothesis:
**position in the performance changes the acceptable amount and type of drum activity.**
It argues against a stationary "same comping density everywhere" policy.

## Emerging drummer-role hypotheses

These are engineering hypotheses to test against score location and verified listening:

1. **Head/entry restraint**
   Many tracks begin with lower attack density. The drummer may establish character
   and pulse without immediately occupying all conversational space.

2. **Solo-development participation**
   Middle regions are, on average, both louder and more transient-active.
   The drummer's role appears to broaden from time support toward interactive shaping.

3. **Non-monotonic narrative**
   Some performances release before the ending; others continue building.
   A drummer therefore needs a narrative state, not a fixed "crescendo through chorus" rule.

4. **Position-conditioned orchestration**
   Higher high-frequency energy in middle/late-middle positions suggests that cymbal
   surface/orchestration may change with development, not just raw hit count.

5. **Restraint remains an action**
   Sparse tracks are not failures of activity. Several regions have low onset rates yet
   large dynamic ranges. The model should allow low-density, high-significance events.
