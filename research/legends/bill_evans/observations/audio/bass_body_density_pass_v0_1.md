# Bill Evans Trio Listening Pass 2 — Bass Body / Density Windows

## Status

Navigation-only study. These measurements come from the mixed compilation and
must not be treated as isolated bass measurements.

The purpose is to identify contrasting windows for repeated listening and score
alignment.

## Priority windows

### BE-001 Waltz For Debby
- opening 0:00–0:40: comparatively sparse low-band attack field
- 1:20–2:00: stronger low-band presence
- 4:20–5:40: much denser low-band event field
- final 0:20: density falls again

Question:
Does the bass become more conversational/directional as the trio opens up later
in the performance, or is the change mostly arrangement/form position?

### BE-002 My Foolish Heart
- opening 0:00–0:20: sparse attacks, weak low-band energy
- around 2:20–3:00: stronger low-band presence and more variable attack strength
- final minute: density reduces again

Question:
How much of the musical weight comes from attack count versus long sounding
body? Compare exact score arrivals with bass decay and silence.

### BE-003 Autumn Leaves
- opening 0:00–0:20: lower event density
- 2:20–2:40: strongest low-band energy region in current scan
- 3:20–4:20: dense low-band event field
- ending: density collapses

Question:
At repeated ii-V / tonic positions, does the bass preserve function while
changing contour, approach type, register, attack and note length?

### BE-004 Blue In Green
- opening 0:00–0:40: sparse low-band field
- around 2:40–3:20: noticeably denser / stronger low-band activity
- ending: very low density

Question:
Is the later density increase a foreground/role shift, or simply stronger
support? Note duration and decay should be annotated before pitch vocabulary.

### BE-010 Nardis
- opening 0:00–0:40: lower density
- around 2:40–3:20: dense low-band activity
- 4:40–5:20: high low-frequency energy without maximum event density

Question:
Does repeated/pedal-like material create structural identity while density and
accent continue to change? This is a key case for productive repetition.

### BE-011 Alice In Wonderland
- opening: sparse low-band field
- around 1:00–1:40: extremely strong low-band presence
- 4:00–5:00: dense event field
- final 0:20: density falls sharply

Question:
How does bass increase activity in 3/4 without losing the waltz floor? Compare
with Waltz For Debby and Someday My Prince Will Come.

### BE-015 Israel
- around 2:40–3:20: strongest low-band energy region
- around 3:40–4:20: high event density
- final 0:20: lower activity

Question:
Which structural arrivals receive extra bass weight, and which chord changes
are intentionally passed through without equal emphasis?

### BE-016 Peri's Scope
- 0:40–2:20: relatively consistent dense low-band field
- final 0:36: reduced activity

Question:
At faster harmonic throughput, does the bass simplify harmonic spelling while
preserving directional line and pulse?

## Cross-track measurements from pass 2

Crude low-band attack/sustain proxies:
- My Foolish Heart: longest median low-band sustain proxy among the priority set
- Blue In Green: also relatively long sustain and high inter-onset variability
- Autumn Leaves: shorter sustain and more regular event flow
- Nardis / Israel / Alice In Wonderland: much stronger low-band share in this
  compilation than Blue In Green or Someday My Prince Will Come
- accent-strength variability is much higher in My Foolish Heart / Blue In
  Green than in Autumn Leaves / Peri's Scope

Interpretation status:
- observation/navigation only
- not instrument-isolated
- not a LegendProfile prior
- must be checked against score/form position and repeated listening

## Score-aligned listening priorities

1. Waltz For Debby:
   compare A/B/C transitions and the later dense region against the written
   sectional form.

2. Autumn Leaves:
   compare identical harmonic-cycle locations across multiple choruses.

3. Blue In Green:
   align sparse opening and denser middle region to the compact harmonic form.

4. Nardis:
   identify repeated grounding cells and determine whether repetition is
   harmonic, rhythmic, registral, or articulation-based.

5. Alice In Wonderland:
   compare activity increase against 3/4 phrase position and piano/drum density.

6. Israel / Peri's Scope:
   test how bass marks form at higher event density without over-accenting every
   harmony change.
