# Bill Evans Repeated Listening / Texture Navigation Pass 01

Purpose: identify large-scale texture/density transition zones for later
score-aligned listening. These observations are navigation aids only and are
NOT yet Bill Evans Legend tendencies.

## BE-001 — Waltz For Debby

Whole-track spectrogram inspection shows several broad texture zones:

- 0:00–~1:10: comparatively lighter upper-spectrum activity and lower overall
  density.
- ~1:10–~3:40: markedly fuller, more continuous broadband activity.
- ~3:40–~5:50: reduced upper-frequency density and a more open overall texture.
- ~5:50–end: density increases again.

Research use: compare these transition zones against score/form position and
foreground ownership before assigning Piano/Bass/Drums roles.

## BE-003 — Autumn Leaves

Broad texture changes are especially visible:

- 0:00–~0:45: moderate continuous activity.
- ~0:45–~2:05: more discontinuous/vertically articulated texture.
- ~2:05–~4:45: sustained dense activity over a broad frequency range.
- ~4:45–~5:15: conspicuous thinning/opening.
- ~5:15–end: fuller activity returns.

Research use: test whether the large thinning around the late-middle section
corresponds to a foreground handoff, formal transition, or dynamic reduction.
Do not infer which instrument owns the change without score-aligned listening.

## BE-004 — Blue In Green

The track shows a substantially more spacious low-density surface than BE-001
and BE-003, with frequent discrete vertical events rather than continuous dense
broadband texture.

- 0:00–~1:05: sparse and clearly articulated events.
- ~1:05–~3:25: increased density and sustained mid/low-frequency activity.
- ~3:25–end: density reduces again while discrete attacks remain visible.

Research use: compare the middle expansion and later reduction against written
form and melody position. This track is useful as a contrast case for
role/density policy because the surface is less continuously active.

## Promotion rule

These observations may guide repeated listening windows, but no statement such
as "Bass solo", "Piano foreground", "Drums drop out", or "Bill Evans tends to..."
is promoted until the audio event is aligned with score/form evidence and the
instrument role is actually verified.
