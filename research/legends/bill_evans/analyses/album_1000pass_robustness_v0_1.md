# Bill Evans Compilation — 1000-Pass Robustness Study v0.1

## Scope

Source: owner-supplied 2:08:37 Bill Evans compilation, segmented into the 24
owner-supplied track boundaries.

This is **not** a claim of 1000 full-duration real-time human listening runs.
It is a 1000-pass parameterized analysis of the same source, followed by
targeted repeated-listening priorities.

1000 passes = 10 analytical families × 100 parameter settings.

The goal is robustness: observations that survive threshold, smoothing, lag,
band-weight and segmentation changes are better candidates for score-aligned
listening than one-off measurements.

Everything below is mixed-audio evidence. It must not be interpreted as
isolated bass measurement.

## Analytical families

1. low-frequency presence — 100 band-weight settings
2. attack-density proxy — 100 normalized threshold settings
3. low-band attack-strength — 100 threshold settings
4. sustain/body proxy — 100 smoothing/tolerance settings
5. accent-variability proxy — 100 strong-event thresholds
6. macro dynamic arc — 100 smoothing windows
7. section contrast — 100 segment-count / low-band-weight settings
8. change activity — 100 smoothing / band-weight settings
9. low/full-band temporal lock — 100 threshold / lag settings
10. repetition/identity proxy — 100 lag / smoothing settings

## Most robust findings

### Nardis — low-frequency identity is exceptionally stable

Nardis ranked at the top of the low-frequency-presence family in essentially
every parameter setting and remained high in low-band attack strength and
change activity.

Interpretation to verify by score-aligned listening:
- strong bass identity may come from register gravity / repeated identity /
  note body as much as note count;
- repetition should not automatically receive anti-template penalties;
- "productive repetition" needs its own musical state.

### Israel — strong low-frequency weight plus strong section contrast

Israel repeatedly ranked near the top for low-frequency presence and
low-band attack strength, and also remained high under section-contrast
segmentations.

Priority question:
does the bass preserve a stable time/harmonic floor while changing weight at
specific structural positions rather than emphasizing every harmony equally?

### Autumn Leaves — sustained low-band body is unusually persistent

Autumn Leaves remained high in the sustain/body family across parameter
settings.

This is useful because the harmonic roadmap itself is recurrent.

Score-aligned question:
at the same ii-V / tonic positions across choruses, which aspects change:
pitch route, register, attack, note body, accent, or interaction role?

### Blue In Green — accent variability and macro dynamic arc survive strongly

Blue In Green remains one of the strongest accent-variability cases and stays
high for macro dynamic arc.

Important implication:
expressive information can be high even when event density is relatively low.

Priority listening dimensions:
- attack weight;
- long decay;
- release timing;
- silence after arrival;
- piano-voicing decay vs bass note body.

### Someday My Prince Will Come — section contrast is extremely robust

Across many segmentation granularities, Someday My Prince Will Come remained
one of the strongest section-contrast cases.

Compare against Waltz For Debby and Alice In Wonderland.

Do not learn a generic 3/4 bass pattern. Learn:
- form-position role;
- phrase-position density;
- accent hierarchy;
- subdivision insertion;
- piano/drum complementarity.

### Alice In Wonderland — low presence + sustained-body behavior

Alice In Wonderland remains high in low-frequency presence and sustain/body
proxies.

This makes it a useful active-waltz contrast:
the bass may become more independent/melodic while preserving a clear meter
floor.

### If You Could See Me Now — strong low-band attacks and internal change

This track repeatedly ranked near the top in low-band attack strength and
change activity.

Priority question:
are these changes aligned with form/foreground handoff, or simply a mix-level
artifact? Do not promote before score alignment.

### Re: The Person I Knew — macro arc, section contrast and repetition identity

This track remained very high in three different families:
- macro dynamic arc;
- section contrast;
- repetition/identity.

This is a strong case for studying how a repeated identity can coexist with
large-scale development.

### Without A Song / You Must Believe In Spring / Gary's Theme

These tracks repeatedly show high macro/identity measures.

They should be used to test:
- long-range arc;
- recurrence with transformation;
- whether phrase development is carried by density, dynamics, register or
  articulation instead of new pitch material.

## Important methodological correction

Not all "high density" measurements mean many bass notes.

This is a mixed recording:
- piano low register,
- kick drum,
- room resonance,
- mastering/EQ

can all contribute to low-band features.

Therefore:
- low-band measures select listening windows;
- they do not become Bass priors;
- bass-specific interpretation requires ear + score/form alignment.

## What survived 1000 passes conceptually

The strongest recurring research directions are:

### 1. Time-floor semantics must be independent from note density

Bass can maintain time through:
- continuous walking;
- sparse metrically decisive anchors;
- waltz grounding;
- repeated/pedal identity;
- long-duration harmonic gravity.

RealSolo should not equate "time carrier" with "quarter notes everywhere."

### 2. Note body is first-class musical information

Attack, sounding length, decay and release need to be modeled alongside pitch.

A correct note with the wrong body can have the wrong musical role.

### 3. Productive repetition must be separated from mechanical repetition

A repeated pitch/role may mean:
- intentional identity;
- pedal/ground;
- form marker;
- ensemble stabilization;

or it may mean:
- scorer laziness;
- repeated template.

Anti-template logic needs evidence of intention/context before penalizing
repetition.

### 4. Form position changes realization even when harmony repeats

Autumn Leaves is the clearest candidate for this study:
same roadmap, different chorus position.

The model should compare repeated harmonic positions across time rather than
treating them as identical situations.

### 5. Accent is relational, not metric-only

Accent meaning depends on:
- piano attack/density;
- drum setup/fill;
- phrase maturity;
- harmonic arrival;
- preceding bass body.

Do not learn a fixed beat-strength table as the main source of bass accent.

### 6. Piano–Bass complementarity is a high-priority latent variable

The 1000-pass study strengthens the case that bass density alone is not enough.

Future aligned annotations should explicitly record:
- piano RH density;
- piano LH density/register;
- bass density;
- bass note body;
- overlap vs complement;
- response delay.

## Next repeated-listening protocol

For each priority score-aligned tune:

1. opening head;
2. first phrase transition;
3. same harmonic location in the next chorus;
4. densest middle section;
5. sparse/release section;
6. out-head / ending.

At each window annotate separately:
- Piano RH role
- Piano LH role
- Bass role
- Drums role
- bass pitch-route family
- register trajectory
- attack weight
- note-body length
- subdivision density
- repeated-identity use
- phrase maturity
- ensemble density
- score/form location
- confidence

Only observations recurring across multiple aligned windows may become
Bill Evans Legend Intelligence.

Only behavior recurring beyond the Bill Evans corpus may be proposed as Shared
Trio Grammar.

## Bottom line

After 1000 parameterized passes, the research signal is stronger, not broader:

**the biggest missing variables in current AI Bass are not more note choices.
They are role, note body, form-position, productive repetition and ensemble
complementarity.**

The next gains should come from understanding when and why the bass changes its
behavior, not from simply expanding its pitch vocabulary.
