## Clark 2014, Massey University

Rowan Clark's phrase/transcription-based analysis of LaFaro in the Bill Evans
Trio reports:

- repeated rhythmic motifs are a recurring device;
- motifs may remain active across multiple bars rather than appearing only as
  isolated ornaments;
- polyrhythmic devices help replace continuous walking accompaniment;
- scalar and arpeggiated material can outline harmony while remaining melodic;
- the study's results caution against assuming that comprehensive two-way
  interactive counterpoint is the dominant behavior at all times.

Runtime implication:
- REPEAT/VARY/DISPLACE may receive modest conditional bias;
- repeated identity must not be automatically treated as template failure;
- melodic linearity may temporarily outrank beat-by-beat root spelling;
- interaction priors should remain context-dependent, not globally maximized.

Reference:
Rowan Clark, *The legendary Scott LaFaro: an analysis of the bass playing of
Scott LaFaro as part of the Bill Evans Trio, 1959-1961*, Massey University /
New Zealand School of Music, 2014.

## Nardis comparative study

A comparative study of Scott LaFaro and Marc Johnson on Nardis reports that
LaFaro develops phrasing through:
- eighth-note and triplet rhythmic variety;
- polyrhythm;
- rhythmic displacement;
- scale and arpeggio patterns that create linear motion through chord changes.

Runtime implication:
- rhythmic subdivision and displacement are first-class solo operations;
- sequence/linear continuation may cross local chord boundaries before
  reconnecting structurally.
