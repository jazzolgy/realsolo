# Bill Evans Trio / Jazz Piano Left-Hand Role Study v0.1

## Purpose

Re-evaluate RealSolo piano left-hand behavior after the observation that:

- comping rhythm was too regular;
- shell/guide-tone-only support was overrepresented;
- piano-led trio sections require RH melody/solo + LH accompaniment coordination;
- in practical jazz piano, one or two tensions are commonly present in LH comping
  voicings when a bassist supplies the root.

This study uses:

1. the uploaded Bill Evans compilation (~2:08:38);
2. user-supplied track timestamps;
3. `master_index.xlsx` to locate available scorebook pages;
4. representative score pages from the uploaded Real/New Real books;
5. published/interview evidence on Evans trio interaction and rootless voicing practice.

The goal is not to clone Bill Evans. The goal is to learn a stronger general jazz-piano
role model from a high-quality reference.

## Audio-pass method

Multiple computational listening passes were run on representative tracks.

For early/middle/late 30-second windows, the analysis measured:

- onset activity;
- RMS variability;
- low / low-mid / mid spectral energy distribution;
- chroma-change activity;
- tempo proxy.

These are ensemble-level measurements, not isolated piano/bass/drum stems.

Representative observations:

### Autumn Leaves

- materially higher onset activity than Blue In Green;
- middle section notably denser than early/late windows;
- harmonic/chroma motion is continuously active.

Implication:
medium-swing functional harmony can support active LH comping, but it should vary
placement and density rather than attack every chord change mechanically.

### Blue In Green

- significantly lower onset activity;
- much higher dynamic variability;
- long low-activity spans;
- strong contrast between sparse and active moments.

Implication:
LH must support long sonorities, inner motion, delayed entries, and silence.
A "one chord symbol -> one attack" model is especially wrong here.

### Alice In Wonderland

- high tempo proxy and strong recurrent low-frequency energy;
- rhythmic activity changes materially through the performance.

Implication:
waltz comping needs asymmetry and phrase response. Beat-1-only or fixed 1-2-3 patterns
should be avoided.

### How Deep Is The Ocean

- active easy/medium swing texture;
- relatively consistent onset activity early/middle;
- lower activity late.

Implication:
LH may be active, but density should track phrase/form state rather than remain fixed.

### Come Rain or Come Shine

- lower tempo / spacious phrasing in much of the recording;
- harmonically dense lead-sheet changes despite relatively open ensemble texture.

Implication:
harmonic complexity does not imply high attack density. Rich tensions can be sustained
with sparse rhythm.

# Score-position comparison

## Autumn Leaves

Lead sheet:

- medium swing;
- repeated functional ii-V / relative-major/minor cycles;
- many long RH melody values;
- clear future harmonic targets.

Piano implications:

- RH owns melody on the head;
- LH should frequently use compact rootless color voicings;
- long melody notes create LH answer windows;
- ii-V motion invites guide-tone continuity plus 9/13 color;
- anticipation is musically plausible;
- not every written chord needs a new LH attack.

Expected LH palette:

- 3rd + 7th + 9th
- 3rd + 7th + 13th
- 3rd + 7th + 9th + 13th
- altered dominant color when explicitly supported
- guide-tone dyad as contrast
- silence

## Blue In Green

Lead sheet:

- unusual compact form;
- slow / floating harmonic rhythm;
- many harmonically colored sonorities;
- long melodic tones and ambiguous-feeling transitions.

External analysis of Evans' voicing language gives a concrete example of an F7 voiced
as 3rd + 13th + b7 + 9th, demonstrating that two color tensions can coexist inside a
compact dominant voicing.

Piano implications:

- 1-2 tensions should be normal, not exceptional;
- LH can sustain while RH shapes melody/line above;
- inner semitone motion and voice-leading matter more than repeated chord attacks;
- rootless color is preferred when bass clearly owns the root;
- root or guide-tone-only gestures remain meaningful as contrast.

## Alice In Wonderland

Lead sheet:

- 3/4;
- continuous functional harmonic motion;
- melody alternates rests, sustained notes and short phrases.

Piano implications:

- RH melody/solo foreground;
- LH rhythm should not become fixed "beat 1 chord + beat 2/3 rest";
- possible LH actions include:
  - anticipation into next bar;
  - beat-2/3 punctuation;
  - delayed support after RH attack;
  - multi-bar sustain;
  - complete lay-out.

Tension use:
compact 3/7 + 9/13 color remains valid, but rhythmic asymmetry is more important than
maximizing chord density.

## How Deep Is The Ocean

Lead sheet:

- easy swing;
- frequent functional changes;
- half-diminished and dominant motion;
- triplet/melodic rhythmic detail.

Piano implications:

- LH must distinguish harmonic identity from rhythmic activity;
- guide-tone continuity through ii-V movement is central;
- extensions can remain while individual voices resolve smoothly;
- chromatic/altered tension should be target-directed, not decorative saturation.

## Come Rain or Come Shine

Lead sheet:

- dense chain of functional and altered dominants;
- multiple opportunities for semitone voice-leading;
- compact form with substantial harmonic color.

Piano implications:

- 9/13 and altered tensions are often more informative than adding roots/fifths;
- the left hand can carry "inner harmony" while RH remains melodic;
- sparse attacks with rich voicings are preferable to frequent plain shells;
- voice-leading continuity should dominate over chord-symbol re-striking.

# General jazz-piano LH role model

## 1. Default with a bassist: color identity, not root duplication

The default LH comping sonority in a piano trio should usually contain:

```
guide-tone identity
+
1 tension
or
2 tensions
```

Examples of abstract role sets:

```
3 + 7 + 9
3 + 7 + 13
3 + 7 + 9 + 13
7 + 9 + 3 + 13
3 + b7 + b9 + 13
3 + b7 + #9 + b13
```

Only use altered colors when Shared Harmony evidence supports them.

This is not a rule that every voicing must have four notes.

## 2. Contrast states are equally important

The LH must also be able to reduce to:

- guide-tone dyad;
- one structural note;
- root/pedal note;
- common tone;
- no note.

These are not inferior voicings. They are texture decisions.

## 3. Root-only is contextual

With an active bassist, root-only LH should be rare but legitimate for:

- pedal points;
- intro/outro;
- section cue;
- dramatic reset;
- bass space;
- explicit unison/structural emphasis.

It must not become routine doubling.

## 4. Voicing should have an internal melodic trajectory

A sequence of LH voicings should not be selected as independent chord grips.

Track:

- top-note trajectory;
- inner semitone motion;
- common tones;
- tension resolution;
- register migration.

The left hand should often feel like a quiet inner melody.

## 5. LH comping rhythm follows the RH phrase

During a piano solo:

```
RH = foreground phrase
LH = responsive harmonic/rhythmic partner
```

LH should be biased toward:

- holes after RH attacks;
- phrase endings;
- syncopated support;
- delayed reinforcement;
- anticipation of the next harmonic target.

Avoid habitual simultaneous RH+LH attacks.

## 6. Silence is a first-class LH action

Published Evans-trio testimony explicitly describes moments with no LH comping at all,
only a RH single line, as well as two-hand octaves and block-chord changes of texture.

Therefore "LH accompaniment" means ownership of the accompaniment role, not continuous
sound.

## 7. Trio role is flexible, not equal-volume democracy

The piano often remains the principal harmonic center, but bass and drums can carry
melodic/rhythmic information that changes what the piano should play.

Bass may:

- walk;
- pedal;
- counterline;
- move to high register;
- temporarily become foreground.

Drums may:

- keep conventional swing;
- thin the pulse;
- answer phrases;
- shape transitions;
- change density independently.

Piano must listen before filling.

# Proposed RealSolo LH states

Instead of one "comping" state, Piano should choose among:

```
LAY_OUT
SINGLE_STRUCTURAL
GUIDE_DYAD
ROOT_PEDAL
COLOR_TRIADIC
ROOTLESS_ONE_TENSION
ROOTLESS_TWO_TENSION
ALTERED_COLOR
SUSTAINED_INNER_VOICE
PUNCTUATION
ANSWER
```

These are role/texture classes, not fixed chord shapes.

Suggested default tendency in piano-led trio:

- ROOTLESS_ONE_TENSION: common
- ROOTLESS_TWO_TENSION: common
- GUIDE_DYAD: occasional
- SINGLE_STRUCTURAL / ROOT_PEDAL: occasional/contextual
- ALTERED_COLOR: context-dependent
- LAY_OUT: frequent enough to preserve conversation

# Important distinction: tension density != rhythmic density

A richer LH voicing does not require more attacks.

RealSolo should be able to produce:

```
few attacks + rich tensions
```

and also:

```
more attacks + very sparse guide-tone material
```

as separate dimensions.

This is a major correction to the earlier comping model.
